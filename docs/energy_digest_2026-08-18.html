0.90 Relevance
Peng Zhang · Aug 17, 2026

Oscillations in renewable energy integrated power systems pose a threat to grid secure operation due to the large-scale integration of renewables. A novel analytical framework explains the mechanism of stimulated oscillations, highlighting the importance of pole positioning and relationships between poles, zeros, and other characteristics on the complex plane. The research finds that its theory surpasses classical stability-based theories rather than negating them.

Why This Matters
This paper's research on stimulated oscillations in renewable energy integrated power systems (REIPSs) matters for grid operators and planners as it provides a deeper understanding of the complex interactions between new renewable sources, which can significantly impact the stability and security of power grids. The findings have direct implications for utility planning and operations, particularly in ISO operations, capacity markets, and NERC standards, as they inform the development of more resilient and adaptive grid management strategies.

DER Allocation without Load Prediction via Reinforcement Learning
0.90 Relevance
Abed AlRahman Al Makdah, Aravind Ramana, Shaofeng Zou et al. · Aug 17, 2026

A reinforcement learning framework for distributed energy resource allocation learns optimal policies directly from operational data, adapting to stochastic demand variations through data-driven updates. The framework models DERA dynamics as a deterministic linear system and exogenous net load as a feature-based linear Markov process, capturing short-range temporal dependencies without explicit forecasting. It achieves high tracking accuracy and stable regulation across heterogeneous DER aggregators without requiring any demand prediction.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it presents a novel approach to address the challenges of grid balancing with variable renewable energy sources, which is crucial for ensuring grid stability and reliability in ISO operations and FERC filings related to renewable integration and capacity markets. The proposed reinforcement learning framework can be applied to real-world scenarios, enabling utility planners to make data-driven decisions for efficient DERA allocation.

Real-Time Control of Sustainable Data Centers: A Two-Layer Model Predictive Control Framework with Workload Flexibility and Heat Recovery
0.90 Relevance
Wenyu Liu, Enea Figini, Mario Paolone · Aug 17, 2026

A two-layer model predictive control (MPC) framework is proposed for real-time control of sustainable data centers, integrating on-site renewable energy, battery storage, waste heat recovery, and district heating. The upper layer employs stochastic optimization to optimize intraday market participation, workload scheduling, and energy management under uncertainty, while the lower layer uses an adaptive MPC strategy to track dispatch references despite short-term disturbances. The framework reduces real-time dispatch deviations and imbalance costs compared to single-layer control strategies, offering a sustainable and grid-supportive operation approach for data centers.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it presents a practical framework for the real-time operation of data centers, which can be directly applicable to utility planners and grid operators seeking to integrate renewable energy sources and manage energy storage systems in their operations. The proposed framework's focus on workload flexibility, heat recovery, and carbon-aware operating signals makes it relevant to capacity markets, ISO operations, and FERC filings.
