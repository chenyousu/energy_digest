Scalable Small-Signal Stability Assessment of Power Systems Based on Frequency-Domain Quadratic Constraints
0.90 Relevance
Xiaoying Liu, Linbin Huang, Hangyu Chen et al. · Sep 28, 2026

Small-signal stability analysis of power systems dominated by power electronics converters faces scalability challenges due to system size increase. A new scalable stability assessment method, based on frequency-domain quadratic constraints, provides quantitative stability indices for multi-converter systems and is shown to clarify requirements for existing conditions. The proposed method reduces conservatism and enables efficient device-level diagnosis with hierarchical screening-diagnosis procedure.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power system engineers as it proposes a scalable method for assessing the small-signal stability of power systems, which is critical for ensuring grid resilience and reliability, especially with the increasing integration of grid-following and grid-forming converters in modern power systems. The developed FQC-based stability condition can be used to optimize device-level diagnosis and certification, directly applicable to utility planning and ISO operations.

0.90 Relevance
M. Furqan Azam, Marta Vanin, Chris Hermans et al. · Sep 28, 2026

A novel neural network framework called Hard-Constrained Probabilistic Factor Graph Neural Network (HCP-PINN) is proposed for distribution system state estimation in the presence of non-Gaussian uncertainty. On the Achievable Inertia Constant of Inverter Based Resources
0.90 Relevance
Christos M. Nikolakakos, Hassan Haes Alhelou, Nikos Hatziargyriou · Sep 28, 2026

The achievable inertia constant of inverter-based resources is bounded by energy reserve, converter power rating with voltage ride-through obligations, and inertia-emulation control scheme activation delay. A unified envelope function combines these bounds into a single value that quantifies the plant's ability to contribute inertia at given loading, grid voltage, and time. The analysis shows that de-loading yields a linear, quantifiable inertia gain and collocated energy storage removes source-coupling constraints, making grid-following plants with storage equivalent post-activation.

Why This Matters
This paper is highly relevant for power system engineers as it provides a unified framework to quantify the achievable inertia constant of inverter-based resources, which is critical for grid resilience and planning, particularly in scenarios involving renewable energy sources and grid-forming control schemes. The results have direct implications for utility planners and grid operators seeking to optimize their systems' response to disturbances and ensure reliable operation.

Frequency Measurement Practices for Inertia Assessment in Inverter Based Resources Dominated Power Systems
0.90 Relevance
Christos M. Nikolakakos, Hassan Haes Alhelou, Nikos Hatziargyriou · Sep 28, 2026

The rate of change of frequency (RoCoF) is measured over tens to hundreds of milliseconds, and the inferred inertia depends on the window size and response delivered during that time. The system inertia is estimated using a closed-form prediction that applies to linear response models, which can inflate the estimate with fast frequency responses. This allows operators to compute their measurement reports and understand the impact of different system configurations on inertia estimates.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for grid operators and utility planners as it provides a crucial understanding of frequency measurement practices, specifically the windowed inertia estimate, which is essential for accurately assessing system inertia in power systems dominated by inverter-based resources, thereby informing their ability to operate the grid effectively and comply with standards and grid codes. This knowledge can be directly applied to ISO operations, FERC filings, NERC standards, and capacity markets.

A Minimum-Order Functional Observer Beyond the Darouach and Luenberger Constructions
0.80 Relevance
Tyrone Fernando · Sep 27, 2026

A general minimum-order functional observer framework has been developed that encompasses both Darouach and Luenberger constructions as special cases. The required functional dimension is determined by a set of observability indices from the triple (A,C,L), and necessary-and-sufficient spectral feasibility conditions ensure this dimension is achievable. The framework enables minimum-order functional observers of intermediate order, even when neither classical observer is available.

Grid-Forming E-STATCOMs for Stable Integration of Large-Scale Data Centers: Modeling and Control
0.90 Relevance
Prabhat Ranjan Bana, Novan Zakkia, Jean-Philippe Hasler et al. · Sep 27, 2026

Grid-forming (GFM) Enhanced STATCOMs (E-STATCOMs) are proposed as a solution for stable integration of large-scale AI data centers into weak or low-inertia networks, providing fast, well-damped responses to abrupt load changes. E-STATCOMs mitigate low-frequency oscillations and interactions with network resonances through tunable dynamic behavior, enabling precise shaping of system response and improved regulation at the point of connection. The proposed approach is scalable and flexible, supported by a design-oriented framework that facilitates parameter selection and performance assessment without requiring extensive EMT studies.

This paper matters to power industry professionals as it addresses a pressing challenge of integrating large-scale data centers into weak grids, which is essential for ensuring the stability and reliability of the grid, particularly in ISO operations where rapid load changes can cause voltage instability and low-frequency oscillations.
