Grid Demand Flexibility Assessment of AI Data Centers via Batch Workload Temporal Shifting
0.80 Relevance
Suntao Su, Liang Du, Shengyi Wang · Sep 29, 2026

The rapid growth of artificial intelligence (AI) data centers poses challenges to power system operation, as their variable and increasing demand requires effective characterization of demand flexibility. A proposed framework assesses the grid-compatible demand flexibility of AI data centers through batch workload temporal shifting, which preserves execution continuity while reducing server power demand. Numerical results show that workload temporal shifting provides quantifiable and grid-compatible demand flexibility with limited disruption to computing workloads.

Why This Matters
This paper's focus on assessing demand flexibility of AI data centers for power system operation is crucial for grid operators, particularly in the context of ISO operations and capacity markets, as it enables more efficient management of variable renewable energy sources and reduces strain on grid infrastructure.

Quantifying EnergyNet performance: a simulation-based framework for decentralized energy networks
0.90 Relevance
Max Collins, Jonas Birgersson, Marc A. Weiss et al. · Sep 29, 2026

A simulation-based framework for decentralized energy networks, called EnergyNet, is developed to model and evaluate the performance of interconnected power electronic routers. The framework represents various components and links within EnergyNet and uses receding-horizon optimization to calculate energy allocation and exchange, as well as support disturbance and reliability analysis. A case study on a Swedish EnergyNet system shows that it can maintain full service even in winter conditions with one grid interface or link lost, but reliability screening indicates that losing two links could partition the network.

Why This Matters
This paper's simulation-based framework for decentralized energy networks, specifically EnergyNet, is highly relevant to power system engineers as it offers a quantitative approach to modeling and evaluating the performance of decentralized energy systems, which can play a crucial role in enhancing grid resilience and reliability, especially in the context of extreme weather events or infrastructure failures. The practical significance lies in its potential application in utility planning, grid operations, and disaster recovery scenarios.

Attack-Resiliency Analytics for Wide-Area Control Systems in Smart Grids
0.90 Relevance
Mohammad Zakaria Haider, Prabin Mali, Nur Imtiazul Haque et al. · Sep 29, 2026

Wide-area monitoring, protection, and control systems in smart grids are vulnerable to false data injection (FDI) attacks due to reliance on synchronized PMU measurements. An attack-resiliency analytics framework is presented that models the dynamics of these systems and formulates an optimal stealthy FDI attack as a mixed-integer linear program. This approach can reduce the attack objective by 15.5-55% and prevent over-frequency relay trips, while still allowing for feasible stealthy attacks.

Why This Matters
This paper's development of an attack-resiliency analytics framework for wide-area control systems is directly relevant to power system engineers, particularly those involved in grid operations and planning, as it can help mitigate the risks of false data injection attacks on grid stability and prevent over-frequency relay trips, which are critical considerations in ensuring the reliability and efficiency of the grid.

0.90 Relevance
Md Obaidur Rahman, Vassilis Kekatos · Sep 29, 2026

A fixed-point framework reuses a single machine learning model trained on basecase topology data to predict post-contingency AC power flow states under non-critical single-line outages, offering a faster and more efficient approach than existing methods.
