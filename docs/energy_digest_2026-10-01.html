Learning Higher Order DC-DC Converter Control from Inversion
0.80 Relevance
Kamakshi Tatkare, Ufuk Topcu, Brian Johnson · Sep 30, 2026

A simple neural-network-based controller learns to regulate high-order DC-DC converters by approximating a state-to-duty control law from model-based bounded inversion trajectories. The proposed method achieves effective regulation under load and input-voltage disturbances without introducing excessive model complexity. It is validated on fourth order Cuk, SEPIC, and zeta converters.

Why This Matters
This paper's development of a neural-network-based controller for high-order converters with nonlinear dynamics is relevant to power system engineers as it can improve the regulation and resilience of grid operations, particularly under load and input-voltage disturbances, which are crucial considerations in ISO operations and utility planning. The method's application to fourth-order Cuk, SEPIC, and zeta converters also makes it useful for energy storage and markets, where the integration of renewable sources poses challenges for grid stability and control.

Duration-Aware Ramp Adequacy Screening
0.90 Relevance
Qian Zhang, Aidan Looney, Chao Tian et al. · Sep 30, 2026

A duration-aware ramp adequacy screening method evaluates whether committed and dispatched fleets can meet anticipated net-demand ramps across different durations, identifying insufficient-duration sets with inadequate ramp capability. This method supports product-duration selection and provides a metric for assessing ramp adequacy over time. The framework also enables forecast-free ramp-reserve scarcity dispatch policies that achieve optimal operational security loss in transmission-unconstrained settings.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power system engineers as it provides a framework for evaluating ramp adequacy and developing forecast-free ramp-reserve scarcity dispatch policies, which can help optimize grid operations and improve the reliability of regional electricity markets, particularly in the context of renewable integration and variable energy sources. The proposed methodology can inform utility planning and capacity market design to ensure secure and efficient operation of power systems.

Flatness-based Neural Network Control of DC-DC Converters
0.80 Relevance
Kamakshi Tatkare, Ufuk Topcu, Brian Johnson · Sep 30, 2026

Researchers present a method that combines model-based trajectory generation with policy learning to control power converters using neural networks, exploiting the analytical structure of converter dynamics to improve efficiency and stability. Parameter-Robust Sensorless Control of IPMSM Drives With Adaptive Flux Observer
0.80 Relevance
Fobao Zhou, Zhenxiao Yin, Xueyan Wang et al. · Sep 30, 2026

A parameter-robust control framework is proposed to address sensitivity issues in IPMSM drives by extending an adaptive flux observer to salient-pole machines. The framework compensates for parameter mismatches using a scalar adaptive flux update and introduces a high-frequency q-axis voltage injection method to identify the q-axis inductance. Experimental results show that the method does not require precise resistance, d-axis inductance, or flux values.

Why This Matters
This paper's parameter-robust sensorless control framework for IPMSM drives is highly relevant to grid operations and resilience, as it addresses a common challenge in motor control systems used in power systems, enabling more efficient and reliable operation of motors in various applications such as renewable energy integration and grid-scale energy storage. The proposed method can contribute to improving the stability and performance of power systems by reducing the impact of parameter mismatches and uncertainties on motor control.

Transient Triggering Grid-Forming Synchronization Control Under Voltage and Frequency Dips
0.90 Relevance
Dewan Mahnaaz Mahmud, Vinu Thomas, Bogdan Marinescu et al. · Sep 30, 2026

Transient Triggering Grid-Forming Synchronization Control uses two feedback signals, terminal voltage and power difference, to adjust internal angle generation in GFM inverters, acting as a virtual braking mechanism to limit angle acceleration during disturbances. The proposed method enhances synchronization stability under voltage and frequency dips without requiring additional supervisory signals or parameters. It is benchmarked against state-of-the-art schemes and validated through high-fidelity electromagnetic transient simulations.

Why This Matters
This paper is highly relevant to power system engineers as it addresses a critical limitation of grid-forming inverters, which are becoming increasingly important in modern power systems, especially under severe disturbances such as voltage and frequency dips. The proposed TTGFM synchronization control method can improve the stability and reliability of grid operations, particularly in applications such as ISO operations and capacity markets.

Assessing Modeling Fidelity for Long-Term Battery Energy Storage Planning: Operation, Degradation, and Temporal Representation
0.90 Relevance
Hassan Zahid Butt, Xingpeng Li · Sep 30, 2026

Long-term battery energy storage system (BESS) planning relies on simplified representations, but their impact on lifecycle conclusions is not well understood. A 20-year grid-connected microgrid study found that omitting calendar aging eliminates replacements, understates lifecycle cost by 35.1%, and a linear degradation surrogate model reproduces replacement years while limiting cost deviation to 0.2%. Modeling fidelity depends on the lifecycle outcome being preserved, with varying results from different representations.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it assesses the modeling fidelity needed for long-term battery energy storage system planning, providing insights into lifecycle conclusions that can inform utility planners and grid operators in evaluating the optimal design and deployment of BESS systems, particularly in renewable integration contexts such as capacity markets and FERC filings.
