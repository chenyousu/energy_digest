Safe Control of Semi-Explicit Differential-Algebraic Systems: Control Barrier Functions with SOS Verification
0.80 Relevance
Mohamad H. Kazma, Hongchao Zhang, Abdallah A. Albustami et al. · Oct 07, 2026

A new control framework for semi-explicit differential-algebraic equation systems uses control barrier functions with sum of squares verification to ensure safety while considering compatibility with algebraic constraints, addressing a limitation of existing methods that fail for such systems due to incompatibility requirements. This approach constructs projected vector fields on the constraint manifold and provides SOS verification conditions. The framework has been validated through case studies on power system applications.

Why This Matters
This paper's development of DAE-aware control barrier functions for semi-explicit differential-algebraic systems can enhance the safety and stability of power grid operations, particularly during renewable integration scenarios or under extreme weather conditions, where dynamic and algebraic states interact. The framework can help grid operators better manage complexity and ensure reliable grid operation.

Learning Stability of Replay-Based Co-Optimization for Transmission Expansion under Strategic Bidding
0.80 Relevance
Tomonari Kanazawa, Hikaru Hoshino, Eiko Furutani · Oct 07, 2026

Learning-based co-optimization for transmission expansion under strategic bidding uses deep reinforcement learning with coupled and non-stationary learning dynamics, leading to inconsistent cost-capacity samples that bias updates. Introducing replay-based capacity updates with nearest-neighbor filtering mitigates this issue but can introduce oscillatory behavior when replay memory is enlarged. A trade-off between replay-memory size and learning rate must be found to achieve stable co-optimization in transmission expansion.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power system engineers as it addresses the challenges of co-optimization in transmission expansion, particularly under strategic bidding, which is crucial for grid resilience and stability. The authors' findings on the trade-off between replay-memory size and learning rate provide practical guidance for utility planners and energy market analysts to improve the robustness and convergence of their optimization processes.

A Single-Inductor N:1 Resonant Switched-Capacitor Ladder Converter with Efficient Continuous Voltage Conversion Capability
0.80 Relevance
Sandeep Reddy Kukunuru, M Mahmudul Hasan Sajeeb, Rohit Reddy Karnaty et al. · Oct 07, 2026

A single-inductor N:1 resonant switched-capacitor ladder converter achieves efficient continuous voltage conversion below the native N:1 resonant ratio while maintaining high efficiency, utilizing a single inductor and operating the bottom stack of switches as diodes to minimize circulating currents. The topology can be applied to any N-to-1 dc-dc conversion application with peak efficiency reaching 97%. A hardware prototype validates the performance, achieving an efficiency >90% while generating a 9V USB-compatible voltage from a 40V-to-60V input.

Why This Matters
This paper's innovative single-inductor N:1 resonant switched-capacitor ladder converter offers efficient continuous voltage conversion capability, making it relevant for grid operators and utility planners aiming to improve power system resilience and efficiency in the face of increasing renewable energy integration and smart grid demands. Its potential applications align with NERC standards and ISO 80000-4 guidelines for energy management systems.

NeuralBES: A Differentiable, Control-Aware Emulator for Scalable Building Energy Modeling
0.80 Relevance
Ting-Yu Dai, Takuya Kurihana, Wing Yee Au et al. · Oct 07, 2026

NeuralBES is a differentiable emulator that resolves the tradeoff between high-fidelity physics simulators like EnergyPlus and data-driven sequence models by parameterizing a thermal model with a shared neural encoder, making it accurate while scaling. Trained on the ResStock dataset across three climate zones, NeuralBES handles heterogeneous building archetypes without requiring per-building calibration. It achieves accuracy comparable to raw-error baselines while operating at significantly fewer parameters than transformer and recurrent baselines.

Why This Matters
This paper's focus on scalable building energy modeling and demand-side flexibility is crucial for grid operators to accurately forecast and manage energy demands, ultimately enhancing the stability and resilience of the power system. The authors' NeuralBES emulator can help improve the accuracy of energy forecasting, which is essential for optimizing grid operations, including ISO operations and FERC filings.

0.80 Relevance
Arnav Bagga, Saskia A. Putri, Abhishek Banerjee et al. · Oct 07, 2026

A new framework using "trajectory sensitivity" is presented to assess the dynamic response of an AI data center under contingencies, providing faster and more accurate analysis than traditional methods. The model captures the local effect of various disturbances on system performance, allowing for the estimation of system trajectories under simultaneous parameter perturbations. This approach reduces computational time from seconds to milliseconds, making it a potential scalable screening tool.

Why This Matters
This paper's trajectory sensitivity-based dynamic assessment framework can help grid operators and planners evaluate the resilience of AI data centers to various disturbances, which is crucial for ensuring the reliability and stability of modern power grids, particularly in regions with high penetrations of renewable energy sources. The study's findings have practical significance for utilities planning and operations, including capacity market analysis and ISO operation evaluations.

AI data centers are creating significant power-electronic loads that affect power system stability and renewable energy integration, with annual electricity consumption not capturing their full impact. However, computational energy efficiency does not necessarily reduce peak demand, electrical disturbances, or carbon emissions, highlighting the need for new research on dynamic load models, high-bandwidth telemetry, and grid-aware scheduling.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it addresses the critical challenge of integrating large-scale AI data centers into power grids, highlighting opportunities and challenges for grid resilience and stability. The practical implications include informing utility planning, optimizing renewable energy integration, and developing strategies to mitigate electrical disturbances in modern power systems.
