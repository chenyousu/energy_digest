The actual effective bifaciality of a 50 MW bifacial PV plant was found to be 0.610 on a time-weighted basis and 0.599 on an irradiance-weighted basis, representing a 13-15% reduction from the nominal bifaciality factor. Shadow Pricing in Transient Stability-Constrained Optimal Power Flow
0.90 Relevance
Alex Coelho, Araceli Hernandez, Luis Badesa · Oct 13, 2026

Shadow pricing in power systems reveals how stability constraints alter economic dispatch, producing price signals and settlement implications that remain poorly characterized. Shadow prices derived from transient stability-constrained optimal power flow decompose into nodal energy and stability components, allowing for asset-specific analysis. These prices enable fair settlement without side payments by charging assets for reduced stability margins and paying those that improve them.

Why This Matters
This paper's contribution to understanding and quantifying the impact of transient stability constraints on power system economics is crucial for grid operators and market analysts, as it can inform the design of more resilient and efficient power systems, and help allocate costs associated with stability constraints in a just and fair manner. The results can also be applied to capacity markets, ISO operations, and utility planning, leading to more informed decision-making.

Impact of Security Modeling Fidelity on Hyperscale Data Center Hosting Capacity Assessment
0.90 Relevance
Haoxiang Wan, Linhan Fang, Jonghwan Kwon et al. · Oct 13, 2026

This paper assesses the impact of security modeling fidelity on hyperscale data center hosting capacity in transmission grids, finding that the N-SCUC model yields an average hosting capacity of 444 MW, while omitting N-1 security results in a 19.8% overestimation. Other models, including DC-OPF and basic SCUC, further overestimate hosting capacity. The impact of unit commitment, reserve requirements, and N-1 security on bus-level hosting capacity estimates is quantified.

Why This Matters
This paper is highly relevant to power system engineers and grid operators, as it assesses the impact of security modeling fidelity on hyperscale data center hosting capacity, which is a critical consideration for utilities and grid planners when evaluating the stability and reliability of the grid. The findings can inform the design and operation of grid infrastructure to accommodate increasing data center loads and mitigate operational constraints.

Constrained Grid-Forming Control for Photovoltaic Systems: A Control Barrier Function Approach
0.90 Relevance
Amir Afshari, Dominic Groß · Oct 13, 2026

The proposed control method for grid-forming PV systems uses control barrier functions (CBFs) to account for constraints of voltage source converters (VSCs) and PV resources, ensuring reliable and safe operation under grid faults, sudden changes in irradiance, and load perturbations. The control combines dual-port grid-forming control with CBFs to restrict its dynamics within the VSC and PV limits. A real-time optimization process is used to minimize a cost function, making the least modification to the control state to remain within the safe set encoded by the CBFs.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it presents a practical solution for integrating photovoltaic systems into the grid, addressing key challenges such as voltage source converter constraints and grid faults. The proposed control barrier function approach can be directly applied to utility planning, renewable integration, and grid operations to ensure reliable and safe operation of GFM PV systems, particularly in ISO operations and FERC filings.
