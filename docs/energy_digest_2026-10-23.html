Grid-interactive buildings can adjust demand, but the extent of this flexibility depends on feeder-head operating requirements, with 24.51% of flexibility contracts rejected due to constraints. Archive coverage constrains abstaining retrieval for transient stability screening more than conditional accuracy
0.80 Relevance
Gregor Cerar, Denis Sodin, Matjaž Škrlec et al. · Oct 22, 2026

Simulating power-grid transient stability is constrained by the availability of historical data in the archive, which affects the performance of estimators, particularly when using retrieval-based methods that rely on archived data. The accuracy of these estimators varies depending on the archive's coverage, with more comprehensive archives like BUS39 showing better performance than less comprehensive ones like ELES. Archive availability constrains the deployment of estimators more than conditional accuracy on some grids.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it proposes a faster critical clearing time estimator for transient stability screening, which can improve the reliability and efficiency of power grid operations, particularly in scenarios with high renewable integration and variable wind/payload inputs, such as ISO operations and FERC filings. By reducing the need for repeated simulations, this estimator can help grid operators and planners make more informed decisions, ultimately contributing to a more resilient and efficient power grid.

Barrier Function Control for the Interconnection of DC Electronic Loads to Utility Grid
0.90 Relevance
Grigoris Michos, Christos K. Verginis · Oct 22, 2026

A nonlinear control law is proposed to safely integrate DC electronic loads to the utility grid, using reciprocal barrier functions to guarantee operation within a desired region despite converter dynamics and fluctuating power demand. The proposed controller ensures the safety of electronic components from power surges and high transients. The controller's performance is validated through simulation scenarios, showing improved stability and robustness compared to conventional saturated-PI controllers.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it proposes a novel control law to ensure safe integration of DC electronic loads into the utility grid, addressing a critical aspect of renewable integration and grid resilience, particularly relevant for utility operators and grid planners aiming to manage variable renewable energy sources and ensure grid stability.

Model Predictive Control of Wind Turbines: A Model-Oriented Review of Prediction Models, Control Objectives, and Nonlinear Aerodynamic Formulations
0.80 Relevance
Roya Khalili-Amirabadi, Mohsen Jalaeian-Farimani, Mahdi Ghane · Oct 22, 2026

Model predictive control (MPC) has been applied to wind turbine regulation using various internal prediction models, including linearized and nonlinear, aeroelastic, and data-driven predictors. Studies show that low-order linear or piecewise-affine predictors are suitable for constrained power and speed regulation, while nonlinear and flexible models are needed to address drivetrain twist, tower motion, and blade loads. A classification system is proposed for direct nonlinear applied-pitch propagation, highlighting the integration challenge at the intersection of multiple prediction, control, and load objectives.

Why This Matters
This paper is relevant to power system engineers as it addresses the application of Model Predictive Control (MPC) in wind turbine regulation, which is crucial for optimizing the performance and integration of wind power into the grid. The research has practical implications for grid operators and renewable integration specialists, as it explores the use of different prediction models, control objectives, and nonlinear aerodynamic formulations to improve the efficiency and reliability of wind power systems.
