Grid-Forming AC/DC Converters for Meshed DC Networks via Dual-Measurement Frequency Droop
0.80 Relevance
Hisham Mahmood, Marcelo A. Elizondo · Oct 26, 2026

A dual-measurement frequency-droop controller enables grid-forming operation in meshed MVDC networks using only two local DC measurements, and maintains stable operation even in regimes where PLL-based grid-following control loses damping. The controller allows for effective DC droop at every synchronized terminal, resulting in a unique steady operating point. It also provides design-use envelopes to map droop ratios and net DC-injection levels into bounds on DC-voltage deviation and terminal currents.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power system engineers as it proposes a new controller design that enables grid-forming operation in meshed DC networks, which is crucial for integrating high-power DC loads like data centers and ensuring grid resilience and stability. Its practical significance lies in the development of innovative control strategies for next-generation power systems.

Symbolic Direct Construction of Augmented Systems for Levenberg-Marquardt Power Flow
0.90 Relevance
Tianshi Cheng, Venkata Dinavahi · Oct 25, 2026

The Levenberg-Marquardt power flow (LMPF) method has been improved upon by introducing a new formulation with a symbolic-permutation-based Jacobian operator, which fills the augmented blocks directly and enables fixed-order, pivot-free factorization. This reformulation results in significant speedups, with direct assembly being 7.5-11.9x faster than the fastest coordinate-list path and 18.0-58.8x faster than cached normal-equation sparse-product formation. The new method also exhibits fast quadratic convergence throughout its tested solvable loading range.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it presents a novel approach to solving the Levenberg-Marquardt power flow problem, which is crucial for optimizing power system operations, resolving ill-conditioned equations, and addressing infeasible operating requests in grid operations. Its proposed method can significantly improve the efficiency and accuracy of power flow calculations, especially in scenarios involving complex grid topologies and renewable energy integration.

Reinsertion-Aware Parameter Estimation of the WECC Composite Load Model via Closed-Loop Equivalent-Network Co-Estimation
0.90 Relevance
Jong Ha Woo, Victor Daldegan Paduani, Zishuo Yang et al. · Oct 25, 2026

Measurement-based parameter estimation of the WECC composite load model neglects the dynamic interaction between the load and grid, causing large errors when the estimated model is reinserted into grid simulations. A new closed-loop framework alternately estimates the CMLD and a five-parameter equivalent network, restoring load-grid interaction and achieving higher reinsertion accuracy than four baseline methods. This approach reduces errors by 73% for active power, 64% for reactive power, and 62% for voltage.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it proposes a new method for parameter estimation of the WECC composite load model, which can significantly improve the accuracy of reinsertion into dynamic grid simulations. This can lead to more reliable and efficient grid operations, particularly in the context of renewable integration and grid resilience.

0.70 Relevance
Sicheng Gong, Zeyu Liu, Danyang Xu et al. · Oct 26, 2026

The proposed hybrid vertex pruning approach can effectively compress security region data, reducing SR data size by at most 96.0% and hypervolume loss to at most 1.65%, making the 8-dimensional SR computationally tractable. FedAvgIm: Federated Learning for Multi-Site Impedance Identification of Inverter-Based Resources
0.80 Relevance
Quang Manh Hoang, Guilherme Vieira Hollweg, Akhtar Hussain et al. · Oct 26, 2026

FedAvgIm, a privacy-aware federated-learning framework, is proposed for multi-site dq-admittance identification of inverter-based resources. Each site trains a local neural network with a small set of operating-point-frequency samples, sharing only model parameters with a cloud aggregator for sample-size-weighted FedAvg updates. FedAvgIm achieves performance close to centralized training, outperforming local-only learning and raw-data-locality-preserving transfer-learning baselines.

Why This Matters
This paper's focus on impedance identification and its application to inverter-based resources is directly relevant to power system engineers, particularly those involved in grid operations and resilience. The proposed FedAvgIm framework can help improve the stability and performance of power systems by enabling more accurate and efficient identification of small-signal interaction dynamics.

Moving bits instead of watts: Accelerating global AI data-centre deployment at the energy-digital-water-land nexus
0.80 Relevance
Jiawei Qu, Xiaoying Shi, Kai Hou et al. · Oct 26, 2026

An AI framework that routes computational workloads through fibre-optic networks rather than transmitting electricity can bypass grid constraints and accelerate global AI data-centre deployment, lowering construction costs by 44-47% and carbon emissions by 38%. This approach avoids 34-67 GW of high-voltage transmission capacity and could bring over 95% of connection-delayed data centres online by 2030. The framework identifies energy-abundant regions and routes data transfer, potentially shifting the global data-centre development landscape.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it provides a novel approach to accelerate global AI data-centre deployment by bypassing grid transmission constraints, potentially alleviating connection queues and enabling more efficient and resilient grid operations. The methodology's focus on leveraging global fibre-optic networks to route computational workloads could inform grid operators and planners in optimizing their grid infrastructure and capacity planning.
