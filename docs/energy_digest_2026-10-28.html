Co-Optimization of Network Topology and Variable Impedance Devices under Dynamic Line Ratings Using Iterative McCormick Relaxation
0.90 Relevance
Junseon Park, Hyeongon Park, Rahul K. gupta · Oct 27, 2026

A unified co-optimization framework integrates Network Topology Optimization (NTO), Dynamic Line Rating (DLR), and Variable Impedance Devices (VIDs) to alleviate congestion and minimize operational costs. The framework uses a McCormick relaxation approach to linearize bilinear terms, achieving improved tractability and solution accuracy. It is validated on the IEEE 118-bus system and additional benchmark systems, demonstrating its effectiveness in improving solution quality and computational efficiency.

Why This Matters
This paper matters to power system engineers as it presents a unified co-optimization framework for Network Topology Optimization, Dynamic Line Rating, and Variable Impedance Devices, which can help alleviate congestion and minimize operational costs in power transmission grids, thereby improving grid resilience and efficiency in the face of increasing renewable integration and changing weather conditions.

Thermal-Mass Coupled Modeling and Optimal Scheduling of Aluminum Smelting Loads for Safe Demand Response Participation
0.80 Relevance
Yan Shen, Ruike Lyu, Shuoqi Wang et al. · Oct 27, 2026

Thermal-Mass Coupled (TMC) modeling is proposed to address operational safety constraints in aluminum smelting demand response participation, capturing complex physical mechanisms of electrolytic cells. The model achieves net economic improvements 122.4% and 325.1% higher than the Equivalent Thermal Parameter (ETP) model in illustrative case studies. The proposed framework supports deeper demand response participation while explicitly assessing operational safety.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it proposes a new approach for optimizing aluminum smelting loads, a flexible resource that can help utilities and grid operators manage demand during peak periods. The results can inform ISO operations, FERC filings, and NERC standards related to demand response participation and grid resilience.

Noise Steering: Exploiting Wake Steering and Acoustic Directivity for Noise-Constrained Wind Farm Control via Reinforcement Learning
0.80 Relevance
Martín de Frutos, Baptiste Corban, Claire Bizon Monroc et al. · Oct 27, 2026

Noise Steering, a model-free reinforcement learning strategy, jointly controls turbine yaw angles and power setpoints to maximize wind farm power while satisfying a local noise constraint, achieving an average power deficit of 2% and up to 7.7% relative to unimpeded control. The strategy outperforms industry-standard noise-reduced operation, with an average power loss of 17%, and retains performance in higher-fidelity simulators. It successfully satisfies the 40 dB noise constraint throughout the full range of turbulent winds.

Why This Matters
This paper's proposal of Noise Steering using reinforcement learning is highly relevant for power system engineers, as it addresses a critical challenge in wind farm integration, namely noise mitigation while maintaining optimal power output. This technology has practical significance for utility planners and grid operators, as it can improve the overall efficiency and environmental impact of wind farms, and may be applicable to future renewable energy integration policies and regulations.

A Topology-Aware Neural Network for Fault Location With Missing Measurements
0.90 Relevance
Fan Jiang, Linhan Fang, Xingpeng Li et al. · Oct 27, 2026

The proposed TANFL neural network uses voltage and current phasors, feeder connection relationships, and line parameters to locate faults in radial distribution feeders. It can handle missing measurements by introducing explicit missing data indicators and random bus data block masking. TANFL outperforms a conventional multilayer perceptron and a formula-based localization method in fault location accuracy, especially in scenarios with missing measurements.

Why This Matters
This paper's proposed topology-aware neural network for fault location can improve the efficiency and accuracy of fault detection and isolation in power grid operations, which is crucial for ensuring grid reliability and stability, particularly in the context of integrating renewable energy sources and managing capacity markets. The practical significance of this work lies in its potential to enhance the performance of power grid operations, which is critical for maintaining grid stability and reliability.

Phase-Coordinated Model Predictive Control for AI Training Power Smoothing
0.80 Relevance
Jie Zhu, Ye Tian, Yinliang Xu · Oct 27, 2026

A new model predictive control framework is proposed to mitigate periodic power fluctuations caused by AI training workloads. By coordinating the relative phases of independent training jobs, the method reduces power fluctuations by 74.39% and 46.6% in mean-square power fluctuations and power standard deviation, respectively. The framework updates launch decisions in real-time to minimize delays and maintain scheduling feasibility.

By proposing a phase-coordinated model predictive control framework, the authors aim to improve the smoothness of power supply and reduce the risk of power system oscillations, making it a relevant contribution to grid operations and resilience.
