Grid-Mode-Aware AI Data Center Power Smoothing via Bayesian-Optimized Hybrid Energy Storage System
0.90 Relevance
Sina Mohammadi, Quang Manh Hoang, Marcus Chen I Wada et al. · Nov 02, 2026

The proposed AI data center power smoothing system uses a Hybrid Energy Storage System (HESS) that combines a Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) and a supercapacitor (SC) to mitigate power fluctuations. The system employs a Bayesian Optimization approach to optimize real-time control parameters, ensuring grid stability and reducing extreme power demand fluctuations. The system successfully smooths out raw demand fluctuations to fit within a tight target band, suppressing resonant excitation and maintaining grid stability.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it proposes a novel solution for mitigating the impact of AI data center power fluctuations on grid stability, which is a critical concern for grid operators and utility planners. The proposed hybrid energy storage system and Bayesian-optimized control framework can help ensure grid resilience and stability in the face of increasing demands from data centers and other emerging technologies.

Transformer-guided Variable Projection for Phasor and Fault-Time Estimation Under DDC Offset
0.80 Relevance
Marcus Chen I Wada, Sina Mohammadi, Van-Hai Bui et al. · Nov 02, 2026

A transformer-guided variable-projection estimator is proposed to accurately estimate phasor, DDC parameters, and fault inception time from POW samples, achieving low phasor estimation errors in post-fault windows and online operation. The estimator's mean maximum overshoot (MO) is at most 2.7% on post-fault windows and 2.2% during online operation, outperforming a traditional DFT-based estimator. The proposed estimator reduces phasor estimation errors in DDC offset scenarios.

Why This Matters
This paper's focus on improving phasor estimation and fault-time estimation under DDC offsets is crucial for grid operators to accurately detect and respond to power system disturbances, ensuring grid stability and reliability. The proposed transformer-guided variable-projection estimator has practical implications for utility planners and grid operators to optimize power system operations, particularly in the context of renewable integration and capacity markets.

Adaptive Residual Modeling for Scenario Learning in Probabilistic Load Forecasting
0.85 Relevance
Jinbao Wang, Jun Liu, Shiliang Zhang et al. · Nov 02, 2026

Adaptive Residual Modeling for Scenario Learning (ARMSL) addresses the challenge of capturing rare observations and extremes in power system operations by proposing a two-stage framework. Stage I models environmental responses and load patterns, while Stage II calibrates extreme marginal quantiles using a reference forecast. The framework outperforms baselines in numerical simulations, achieving significant reductions in pinball loss and feeder error rates.

Why This Matters
This paper's focus on adaptive residual modeling for scenario learning in probabilistic load forecasting is highly relevant to power system engineers, as it addresses the need for accurate and reliable load forecasts to ensure grid stability and resilience, particularly in the context of integrating variable renewable energy sources and managing extreme weather events. The proposed framework can inform grid operators' decisions on resource allocation, demand forecasting, and risk management.

Protection challenges and solutions on power systems towards 100% inverter-based resources and energy storage systems
0.90 Relevance
Alexander Novikov, Luis A. Garcia-Reyes, Anup Joshi et al. · Nov 02, 2026

The increasing share of renewable energy sources in power systems, driven by decarbonization efforts, poses challenges for fault protection and reliability in power systems. Existing protection schemes may not provide accuracy and reliability due to limitations in converter controls and the unique characteristics of inverter-based resources, such as limited negative and zero sequence current injection. New understanding and models are needed to address these challenges and ensure the stability and resilience of power systems with high shares of converter-based resources.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it addresses critical challenges in protecting power systems with high levels of renewable energy integration, which is a key aspect of the grid operators' and utility planners' responsibilities. The findings and proposed solutions are directly applicable to ensuring the reliability and resilience of modern power systems, particularly in ISO operations and NERC standards compliance.

A Cooperative Game-Theoretic Framework for Real-Time Deviation Settlement in Local Energy Communities
0.90 Relevance
Angel M. González-Rueda, Iago Núñez Lugilde, Jose L. Crespo-Vazquez et al. · Nov 02, 2026

A cooperative game-theoretic framework is proposed for real-time deviation settlement in Community-Based Local Energy Markets, modeling the process as a transferable-utility cooperative game. Low-rank corrections to a shared reference Jacobian for GPU-parallel AC $N\!-\!1$ contingency analysis
0.90 Relevance
Marc Hunkemöller, Nico Westerbeck, Joost van Dijk et al. · Nov 02, 2026

A GPU-parallel solver for $N\!-\!1$ contingency analysis that uses a single reference Jacobian and low-rank Woodbury corrections to handle topology changes. The method attains competitive solve times with an optimized CPU baseline and has a minimal impact on workload, making it feasible at transmission scale. It achieves this by separating the change in operating state from the change in topology.

0.90 Relevance

The proposed method shows superior performance compared to conventional grid-following control, improving grid stability and addressing challenges posed by high renewable energy penetration.
