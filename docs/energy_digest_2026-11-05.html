Spatial Differences Analysis of Nodal Frequency Considering Frequency-Voltage Coupling
0.90 Relevance
Xiaodan Li, Donghai Zhu, Zining Liu et al. · Nov 04, 2026

Spatial differences in nodal frequency are amplified through frequency-voltage coupling in inverter-based resources, with existing analyses neglecting voltage dynamics or only examining aggregate frequency. A new unified model captures the device-network F-V coupling, revealing how source configuration and parameters modify the effect of amplifying nodal frequency differences. The model's computational efficiency is validated through case studies on two test systems.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power system engineers as it addresses a critical aspect of frequency security in the context of increasing penetration of inverter-based resources, directly relevant to grid operators and utility planners who need to ensure reliable and stable power supply. The proposed model and analysis can inform the development of effective strategies for managing frequency-voltage coupling and mitigating its impact on grid resilience.

Transversal Passivity: Dissipation Relative to Input and Output Manifolds
0.80 Relevance
Feng-Yu Yue, Daniel Zelazo · Nov 04, 2026

Transversal passivity is a local dissipation property that regulates input and output signals to prescribed manifolds, allowing motion along the manifolds without penalty. It provides a necessary and sufficient condition for the property and relates it to existing passivity notions, and can be assessed before closed-loop analysis. The property has implications for power conservation and port convergence in feedback interconnections through negative-adjoint linear channels.

Why This Matters
This paper's concept of transversal passivity can be applied to power system engineers by regulating input and output signals to prescribed manifolds, which can help in managing grid stability and efficiency, particularly in scenarios involving renewable integration and energy storage. This knowledge can be particularly valuable for utility planners and grid operators who need to balance supply and demand, manage energy storage, and ensure grid resilience.

Conflict-Aware Impedance-Sensitivity-Based Tuning for Multi-Mode Damping Enhancement
0.80 Relevance
Quang Manh Hoang, Xuan Thanh Trinh, Sina Zarrabian et al. · Nov 04, 2026

Conflict-aware impedance-sensitivity-based tuning improves simultaneous damping enhancement of multiple modes, converting impedance-derived eigenvalue sensitivities into normalized damping-ratio sensitivities to diagnose tuning directions. A low-dimensional max--min problem maximizes predicted damping of the worst target mode subject to parameter limits and feasibility requirements. This method has been successfully tested on two test systems, demonstrating the value of pairwise coordination.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power system engineers as it presents a practical method for optimizing damping enhancement in power systems, which is critical for ensuring grid stability and reliability. The proposed conflict-aware tuning strategy can be applied to various grid operations, such as ISO operations and capacity market planning, to improve system performance and meet damping targets.

Frequency-Frame Covariance for Reactive-Power Scalarization
0.80 Relevance
Kai Sun · Nov 04, 2026

Measured voltage and current waveforms can be represented in a reference frame with a different frequency than the physical fundamental frequency, but changing the readout operator can alter reactive-power readouts. A new covariance formulation is presented that ensures consistent transformation of the readout operator with the waveform representation. The formulation provides a necessary condition for determining the consistency of the readout operator and separates errors caused by waveform projections from errors due to measurement uncertainty.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power system engineers as it addresses a critical aspect of frequency-frame covariance, enabling accurate reactive-power readouts in a reference frame with a different frequency than the physical fundamental frequency. This is particularly relevant for grid operations, especially in scenarios involving frequency shift due to renewable energy integration, where accurate reactive-power calculations are essential for ensuring grid stability and reliability.

Multi-Period Bilevel Coordination of Primary and Secondary Frequency Regulation for Grid-Forming Battery Energy Storage
0.90 Relevance
Wenju Shang, Liangliang Hao, Jiangliang Jin et al. · Nov 04, 2026

The article presents a method for coordinating primary and secondary frequency regulation in grid-forming battery energy storage systems using a multi-period bilevel program. The proposed method uses a continuation approach to handle numerical difficulties introduced by complementarity constraints, and it is proven to satisfy a first-order stationarity condition. This approach reduces solution time by 89.3% and peak frequency deviation by 0.53% compared to conventional methods.

Why This Matters
This paper's focus on multi-period bilevel coordination of primary and secondary frequency regulation for grid-forming battery energy storage systems is highly relevant for the power industry, particularly for grid operators and renewable integration specialists, as it addresses a critical challenge in integrating variable renewable energy sources into the grid while maintaining grid stability and reliability, with practical implications for optimizing energy storage and market operations.

0.80 Relevance
Iva Bačić, Philipp C. Böttcher, Marc Hunkemöller et al. · Nov 04, 2026

Phase locking in oscillator networks is highly dependent on interaction strengths between coupled units, particularly when couplings are uncertain, state-dependent, or time varying. Rigorous conditions for phase locking are derived using a convex formulation of nonlinear fixed-point equations, yielding edge-wise error bounds for feasible approximations.
