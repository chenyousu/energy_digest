Aeroelastically Constrained Optimal Path Planning of a Fixed-Wing Airborne Wind Energy System
0.80 Relevance
Omid Heydarnia, Jolan Wauters, Tom Lefebvre et al. · Nov 10, 2026

Fixed-wing airborne wind energy systems' optimal path planning may not accurately reflect the physical system's behavior if the wing is not accurately modeled. Accounting for the wing's elastic deformation and twist can reduce the achievable power by up to 31%. Forward simulations show that the quasi-static deflection and twist are close to the dynamic limits, allowing for more accurate trajectory optimization.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it addresses the aerodynamic constraints of fixed-wing airborne wind energy systems, which is a critical component for the integration of large-scale renewable energy sources into the grid. By optimizing the wing deflection and twist, the authors provide insights into the potential power gains and limitations of AWES, informing utility planners and renewable integration specialists on the feasibility and potential of airborne wind energy in the power sector.
