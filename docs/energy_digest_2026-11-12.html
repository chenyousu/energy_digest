Sweden plans new inverter and battery database

Solar
Source: PV Magazine
Published: November 12, 2026

Summary

Sweden plans to replace its existing database of pre-assessed solar and battery inverters with a new digital database called Rikta Rätt 2.0, set to launch in 2027, which will provide standardized technical information on inverters used in solar photovoltaic and battery energy storage installations. The new database will not require additional product testing solely for inclusion and will rely on certificates, test reports, and other documentation from existing procedures. The database will provide technical information and supporting documentation for each model, helping electricity distribution system operators assess grid connection applications.

Information-Theoretic Threshold Design for Sparse Event Encoding in Learning Systems
0.70 Relevance
Pratishtha Shukla, Maksudul Alam, Srikanth B. Yoginath et al. · Nov 10, 2026

The paper establishes a connection between threshold-based event encoding and discretized learning systems, identifying a dimensionless ratio governing information retention, and derives an information-sufficiency boundary using normal-condition data. It also formulates a data-driven criterion that selects the sparsest threshold retaining a prescribed fraction of peak discriminative information. The proposed design improves power-system fault classification performance by identifying sparse operating thresholds.

Why This Matters
This paper's focus on sparse event encoding and threshold design can be applied to power system fault classification, providing valuable insights for grid operators and utility planners in identifying and responding to faults in real-time, thereby enhancing grid resilience and operations. The framework can also be used to optimize capacity market designs and renewable integration strategies.

Physics-Constrained Autoencoder for Interpretable Health Representation Learning in Turbofan Engines
0.80 Relevance
Leonardo Baldo, Jokin Alcibar Ibarzabal, Daniel Cisneros Acevedo et al. · Nov 10, 2026

A physics-constrained autoencoder is proposed to learn interpretable health representations in turbofan engines, enabling component-level analysis and diagnostic value. The model incorporates physical equations in the decoder architecture and learns from a loss function that promotes the representation of predefined physical quantities. The methodology achieves comparable reconstruction performance to fully data-driven models and demonstrates its effectiveness in tracking degradation and producing a health index with high correlation to ground truth.

Why This Matters
This paper matters to power industry professionals as it proposes a novel approach to health representation learning in complex systems like turbofan engines, which can be adapted to improve the analysis and diagnosis of grid-related issues such as power plant degradation, equipment failures, and energy system resilience. The practical application of this technology could enhance grid operations and resilience by enabling more accurate predictive modeling and decision-making.
