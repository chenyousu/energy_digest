GRID-MRI: Resilience Assessment of Power Systems under Coordinated Cyber-Physical Attacks
0.90 Relevance
Isaac Ortega Romero, Ioannis Zografopoulos · Nov 12, 2026

The Grid Multidimensional Resilience Index (GRID-MRI) framework assesses the resilience of power systems under coordinated cyber-physical attacks by integrating endogenous disruption severity, coordination effects, and exogenous stressors. Coordinated attacks induce system collapse, resulting in resilience losses 3.72 to 4.09 times their additive estimates. GRID-MRI provides a quantitative basis for understanding the dominant mechanisms of multidimensional resilience degradation.

Why This Matters
This paper is highly relevant for power system engineers, grid operators, and energy market analysts as it provides a novel framework for assessing the resilience of power systems under coordinated cyber-physical attacks, which is essential for utility planning, capacity market operations, and FERC filings. The findings have significant implications for grid operators to develop strategies to mitigate the impact of such attacks and ensure the reliability of the grid.

Mode-in-Variance Participation Factors under Stochastic Excitation
0.90 Relevance
Bruno Pinheiro, Liya Huang, Georgios Tzounas · Nov 12, 2026

This paper investigates how the dynamic modes of a power system contribute to the stationary variance of its variables under continuous stochastic excitation, and proposes a metric called mode-in-variance participation factors (PFs) to quantify this relationship. PFs allow for the identification of modes governing system variability, quantification of modal interactions, and targeting of control dynamics to reduce stochastic variability. The PFs can be used to analyze and improve the control and stability of power systems.

Why This Matters
This paper's focus on mode-in-variance participation factors can help grid operators identify and manage the effects of modal interactions on system variability, ultimately leading to improved frequency and voltage stability, and enhanced resilience to stochastic disturbances. The findings can inform control strategies and optimization techniques for reducing stochastic variability in power systems, particularly in the context of renewable integration and variable wind/solar resources.

Adjacent Heat Transfer Effects Can Bias Savings Estimates When Controlling Only Part of a Multi-Zone Building
0.80 Relevance
Arash J. Khabbazi, Kevin J. Kircher · Nov 12, 2026

Advanced heating, ventilation, and air-conditioning control systems can overestimate whole-building savings due to heat drawn from neighboring zones being counted as savings. The error can be corrected by evaluating the thermal conductance ratio, with the correction reducing to the ratio of two conductances for slowly changing prices. This correction recovers the true savings and reproduces the measured relative error in simulated building scenarios.

Why This Matters
This paper's focus on controlling thermal zones within a multi-zone building and its implications for whole-building savings estimates is particularly relevant to grid operators and utility planners, as it highlights the need for accurate building performance modeling in ISO operations and FERC filings. The study's results on the impact of adjacent heat transfer effects on energy savings estimates can inform utility planning and resource allocation decisions for more efficient and resilient grid operations.

0.90 Relevance
Jonathan M. Snodgrass, Thomas J. Overbye, Christopher L. DeMarco et al. · Nov 12, 2026

GridForge, a tractable network-flow method, constructs open, continental-scale synthetic transmission grids by keeping every bus pair within physical length limits and selecting lines with a Garver's length-weighted network-flow linear program. The synthetic grids are 78-78,484 buses in size and span across the upper Midwest and Tennessee Valley footprints, with most lines built at 69 kV.
