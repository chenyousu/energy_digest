Quantifying the hosting capacity value of workload flexibility for AI data centers: A two-stage electricity-computation co-optimization framework
0.90 Relevance
Jiakai Wu, Wei Sun, Pengyu Ren et al. · Nov 18, 2026

A two-stage electricity-computation co-optimization framework is developed to quantify the hosting capacity value of workload flexibility for AI data centers, which shows that temporal and spatial flexibility individually increase hosting capacity by 21.26% and 5.37%, respectively, and their coordinated use raises hosting capacity by 34.44% without network reinforcement. The coordinated gain exceeds the sum of the individual gains, demonstrating complementarity between spatial migration and temporal deferral. The framework enables fuller utilization of existing transmission capacity by redistributing computational demand across locations and operating hours.

Why This Matters
This paper is highly relevant to power system engineers, as it addresses the need to quantify the hosting capacity value of workload flexibility for AI data centers, which can help grid operators optimize energy supply and demand in the context of growing demand for distributed energy resources and increasing grid resilience. The findings have direct applicability to grid operations and resilience, particularly in planning and managing peak demand and grid stability.

0.90 Relevance

Low-inertia power systems increasingly rely on Fast Frequency Response (FFR) to maintain frequency stability. Capacity-Aware Redistribution Control for Mitigation of Cascading Failures in Networks
0.80 Relevance
Ujjwal Pratap, Christian A. Hans · Nov 18, 2026

The article proposes a method for controlling irreversible cascading failures in networks with finite node capacities by updating topology matrices to preserve unit row sums and quantify unserved load explicitly, preventing secondary failures and minimizing unserved load while optimizing capacity utilization. The method involves a two-stage optimization problem and has been tested on grid and Barabasi-Albert networks, showing promising results in preventing secondary failures. The approach has a tradeoff against served load on scale-free networks.

Why This Matters
The proposed method is directly relevant to power system engineers, as it addresses the mitigation of cascading failures in networks, a critical issue for grid operators to ensure reliable and efficient operation. This work is applicable to real-world scenarios, such as ISO operations and FERC filings, where preventing secondary failures and optimizing capacity utilization are essential for maintaining grid stability.

N-1 Security constrained Hosting Capacity Assessment for Ultra Large-scale Data Center with Workload Flexibility
0.90 Relevance
Jiakai Wu, Pengyu Ren, Yifan Wang et al. · Nov 18, 2026

A hosting-capacity framework has been developed to assess the impact of transmission network security on large-scale data centers, revealing that workload flexibility can unlock significant capacity gains, with up to 24.5% increase in N-1 hosting capacity through batch deferral. The framework considers computing demand, batch workload scheduling, and N-1 security constraints, and identifies that workload flexibility is a location-dependent connection resource. The analysis shows that six-hour batch deferral can capture 90.9% of the capacity gain, highlighting the importance of considering contingency exposure and deadline constrainted workload recovery.

Why This Matters
This paper matters to power industry professionals as it provides valuable insights on how workload flexibility can be used to increase the hosting capacity of ultra-large-scale data centers under N-1 security constraints, making it directly applicable to grid operations and resilience in the context of capacity markets and utility planning. The findings can inform utility planners and grid operators on how to better manage transmission network security and optimize data center placement.

Rented Oscillations: Multi-Tenant Data Center Workload Synchronization Attacks and Mitigation
0.80 Relevance
Zaint A. Alexakis, Li Zhou, S. Gokul Krishnan et al. · Nov 18, 2026

A stealthy adversary can synchronize their computing workloads to create periodic oscillations in a multi-tenant data center, causing the facility's generators to reach their maximum capacity and leading to potential power grid instability. The oscillations can be detected and mitigated using signal-energy-based detection methods, which involve re-timing the workloads to minimize the facility's oscillatory energy. The approach is validated through experiments and real-time simulations on a surrogate model of the Saudi Arabian transmission network.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it highlights a new type of attack on power systems, specifically in the context of multi-tenant data centers, which can lead to oscillations in facility consumption. Understanding and mitigating such attacks is crucial for grid operators and utility planners to ensure the stability and reliability of the power grid.
