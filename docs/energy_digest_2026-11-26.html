Grey-Box Experimental Identification of Reduced-Order Models for Grid-Forming Converters
0.80 Relevance
Marcelo Cardenas, Stephan Kenzelmann, Matteo Saviozzi et al. · Nov 25, 2026

A grey-box system identification method is proposed for identifying reduced-order models of grid-forming converters, with three techniques being compared: balanced truncation, singular perturbation with frequency cutoff and participation factors, and singular perturbation with energy residuals. The results show that balanced truncation achieves the highest time-domain fit, but may not provide the most reliable representation of modal properties for small-signal stability. Singular perturbation with energy residuals offers a good compromise between accuracy, physical interpretability, and modal preservation.

Why This Matters
This paper is relevant for grid operators and utility planners as it proposes a grey-box system identification method for reduced-order models of grid-forming converters, which can improve the accuracy and reliability of grid modeling, ultimately enhancing the stability and resilience of power systems. This finding has practical implications for the design and operation of grid-forming converters in renewable energy systems, such as those involved in ISO operations and FERC filings.

Decentralized Control Barrier Function Filters for Frequency Containment With Inverters
0.80 Relevance
Damola Ajeyemi, Emiliano Dall'Anese · Nov 25, 2026

Decentralized control barrier function filters are designed to supplement primary frequency control and constrain frequency nadir and zenith following generation-demand imbalances in power transmission systems. These filters are implemented at each inverter, using local frequency and rate-of-change-of-frequency estimates, and are validated on the IEEE 39-bus system using a high-fidelity electromagnetic-transient model. The filters are designed to render the set of admissible frequencies forward invariant, providing bounds on the distance between state trajectories.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it addresses the need for decentralized control systems to mitigate frequency containment issues in power transmission systems, which is crucial for the widespread integration of renewable energy sources and ensuring the stability of the grid. The proposed method can be directly applied to real-world power systems, enabling more efficient and effective frequency regulation and enhancing grid resilience.

Travelling-Wave-Based Protection of DC Hubs in Bipolar HVDC Grids
0.90 Relevance
Rahul Rane, Victor Daniel Reyes Dreke, Wilhelm Winter et al. · Nov 25, 2026

A stationary wavelet transform-based method is proposed for detecting and classifying DC faults in a bipolar HVDC grid, using line-mode voltage wavelet detail energy for detection and pole-voltage and DMR current-derived wavelet detail energy for classification. The method is tested using a real-time hardware-in-the-loop platform and demonstrates feasibility for real-time implementation, with fault location and traveling-wave propagation affecting transient energy and protection response time. The method distinguishes between pole-to-pole, pole-to-ground, and pole-to-DMR faults.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it presents a novel method for real-time DC fault detection and classification in bipolar HVDC grids, which is crucial for ensuring grid resilience and stability, particularly in the context of integrating high penetration levels of renewable energy sources. This technology can also support ISO operations, FERC filings, and NERC standards by providing a robust and efficient way to protect and manage the grid's DC infrastructure.

Decision-Focused Learning for Multistage Stochastic Programs, with Application to Power Network Repair Scheduling
0.80 Relevance
David Krame Kadurha, Alessandro Zocca, Sandjai Bhulai · Nov 25, 2026

Decision-focused learning (DFL) is a framework for solving multistage stochastic programs, particularly in power network repair scheduling, by training a neural network on labels from offline single-scenario problems. The framework outperforms baseline policies in certain scenarios, achieving cost reductions of 14% to 23% under a binding budget, and is computationally efficient, with decision times comparable to trivial heuristics and orders of magnitude faster than model-predictive control (MPC). DFL can be applied to other multistage stochastic programs with similar structural conditions.

Why This Matters
This paper's decision-focused learning framework for multistage stochastic programs can help power system engineers and grid operators optimize repair scheduling for power networks under stochastic, time-varying component failures, potentially leading to improved grid resilience and efficiency, particularly in the context of integrating renewable energy sources.

Passivity Shaping over Frequency Bands: A Differentiable Optimization Approach
0.90 Relevance
Alberto De Luca, Pol Jane-Soneira · Nov 25, 2026

A compositional input-output framework for assessing stability in interconnected grid subsystems uses passivity, which introduces new grid stability challenges due to the energy transition and converter-dominated grids. A novel optimization approach is proposed to tune controller parameters to maximize passivity over specific frequency bands, addressing the current gap in practice. The method is validated on two case studies: a grid-following converter and a multi-converter microgrid.

The proposed method can be directly applied to optimize controller parameters for passivity over specific frequency ranges, enabling grid operators and utility planners to improve grid stability and reliability.

DynaSeek: Large Language Model-Driven Hybrid Dynamic Model Discovery for Grid-Tied Inverters from Limited Measurements
0.80 Relevance
Hongwei Zhen, Chao Shen, Jianfeng Niu et al. · Nov 25, 2026

DynaSeek, a large language model-driven framework, proposes a new method for discovering explicit hybrid differential-algebraic equations of grid-tied inverters from limited measurements. The framework integrates physics-constrained symbolic skeleton synthesis with joint numerical estimation of shared model parameters and record-specific latent initial states. DynaSeek reduces transient trajectory reconstruction errors by one to two orders of magnitude compared to existing methods, enabling reliable large-signal stability assessment of inverters.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it presents a novel framework (DynaSeek) to improve the stability analysis of grid-tied inverters, a crucial component in modern power systems. By developing a large language model-driven approach, the authors aim to reduce transient trajectory reconstruction errors, making it a significant contribution to the field of grid operations and resilience.

0.90 Relevance
Maruthi Vemula, Neeraj Praneeth Gajula · Nov 25, 2026

Replacing a fixed 20% battery reserve with a county-risk threshold increases observed five-hour backup coverage from 75.0% to 86.5%, adding $10.2 in annualized margin per home. A Robust Optimization Framework for Energy System Capacity Expansion under Line Outage Uncertainty
0.90 Relevance
Kamran Forghani, Lina Reichenberg, Fredrik Hedenus et al. · Nov 25, 2026

A robust optimization framework is developed for energy system capacity expansion under line outage uncertainty, allowing for the simulation of thousands of outage scenarios and hedging against the most severe ones. The framework achieves a VaR bound of less than 1% in a 25-node case study, and the robust plans are shown to nearly eliminate loss of load and have a lower mean system cost compared to a deterministic plan and a reserve-margin heuristic. The approach mainly changes the type and location of capacity, rather than adding more generation capacity.

Why This Matters
This paper matters for power industry professionals as it provides a robust optimization framework for capacity expansion under line outage uncertainty, directly addressing the reliability and resilience challenges faced by grid operators in integrating increasing amounts of renewable energy into the grid. The proposed method can be applied to capacity markets, utility planning, and ISO operations to ensure a more secure and efficient power system.

Robust Recourse under Causal Graph Ambiguity
0.80 Relevance
Muyan Anna Li, Juliette Sinnott, Mohammad Kohandel et al. · Nov 25, 2026

Causal algorithmic recourse models how interventions change an individual's features to reverse unfavorable decisions, but existing methods may not account for ambiguity in causal mechanisms. Two new methods, Worst-Case Recourse and Graph-Weighted Recourse, are proposed to find low-cost actions that succeed across multiple candidate graphs or a fraction of graph weight. Relaxing robustness requirements increases the number of valid recommendations for recourse, but may lower the validity of actions.

Why This Matters
This paper's focus on robust recourse under causal graph ambiguity is particularly relevant to power system engineers, as it addresses the challenges of ensuring reliable and resilient grid operations in the presence of uncertainty and ambiguity in system modeling. The proposed methods can help grid operators and planners develop more effective strategies for mitigating the impacts of uncertainty on grid stability and reliability.
